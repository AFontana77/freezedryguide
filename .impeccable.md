## Design Context

### Users
Home freeze-drying enthusiasts, serious preppers, and homesteaders who have already decided freeze-drying is worth exploring. Researching a $2,695–$4,995 purchase (Harvest Right). Typically 30–60, higher income, detail-oriented. Reading on desktop in the evening. Primary job: understand exactly what they're buying before committing to a major appliance purchase.

### Brand Personality
**Three words:** Expert, methodical, unsparing.

Explains what Harvest Right's marketing doesn't tell you: 24–36 hour batch times, 60–70 dB noise, oil pump maintenance, chamber seal replacement. Feels like a technical product review from an engineer who actually owns the machine — not a commission-optimized affiliate article.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light mode (evening research, but content-heavy — dark mode would make dense tables harder to scan).
Clean and technical editorial — structured data-heavy (specs tables, comparison grids, yield tables). Closer to a product teardown blog than a lifestyle site. Should feel authoritative and precise. Warm neutrals rather than cold grays — the subject is food, not code.

Anti-references: avoid lifestyle food photography vibes, colorful infographic-heavy layouts, or anything that feels like a cooking blog. This is a considered purchase guide, not recipe content.

### Design Principles
1. **Full technical transparency** — show the specs and real costs nobody else lists
2. **Data before opinions** — tables and numbers lead, prose explains
3. **High AOV trust bar** — at $3,000+ a purchase, every claim needs to feel earned
4. **Dense but scannable** — section anchors, clear h2 labels, verdict boxes that can be read in 10 seconds
5. **No AI slop patterns** — side-stripe borders (fixed across site), gradient text, and decorative glassmorphism are banned
